Peru

Three landscapes in one country, coast, Andes and Amazon, and five neighbours not to mix up.

Country profile · game data, October 2026

Peru has 34.2 million people according to the game, forty-eighth in the world, on 1,285,216 km², the nineteenth largest territory. Its density is about 27 people per square kilometre, a low figure that geography explains: a desert coastal strip, the Andes in the centre and Amazon rainforest in the east, which covers a large share of the territory and stays thinly populated.

The capital is Lima, on the Pacific, and also the largest city. In a quiz the decoys are Cusco, the former Inca capital, and Arequipa, but neither is the current capital. The game lists three languages for Peru, reflecting Spanish, Quechua and Aymara.

It has five land neighbours. Colombia and Ecuador lie to the north; Brazil to the east; Bolivia to the south-east; Chile to the south. The border with Bolivia runs through Lake Titicaca, which the two countries share. That high-altitude lake is a good anchor for remembering that Peru and Bolivia are neighbours. For the rest of the region, see the South America capitals quiz.

The flag is made of three vertical bands, red, white and red, like Canada’s, from which it differs by having no maple leaf in the centre in its civil version (the state version carries the coat of arms). If the flag is striped vertically with no symbol, think Peru; with a leaf, it is Canada.
