How it works

A country appears, you pick its capital from four options. The wrong answers are not random: most often they are the country's largest city, or a neighbour's capital. That is what makes the mode instructive rather than mechanical — it goes straight at the confusions people actually hold.

The score depends on correctness and response time, on a 0–1000 scale shared by every mode. A run of quick answers pushes the total up faster than a series of laboured correct ones.

As with flags, a solo run can be limited to one continent. That is the most efficient way to handle a blind spot: almost everyone knows Europe and almost nobody knows the Caribbean.

The mode also feeds the daily challenge and online duels, where both players get exactly the same questions.

Frequently asked

What is the capital of Australia?
Canberra, not Sydney. The city was built between Sydney and Melbourne, which were fighting over the title in 1908. It is the most common trap in the mode.
How many capitals does the quiz cover?
{{countries}}, one per country in the game. States with multiple or contested capitals follow atlas convention: Pretoria for South Africa, Sucre for Bolivia, Amsterdam for the Netherlands.
Can I play without an account?
Yes. The daily challenge starts straight in the browser. A free account is only for keeping your progress and playing online.
Can I revise one continent?
Yes: solo play lets you restrict a run to a single continent.
