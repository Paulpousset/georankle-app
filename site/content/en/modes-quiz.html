Geography quiz: flags, capitals, shapes and clues

Five ways to test yourself on the world’s {{countries}} countries, from four-option questions to pure deduction. All free, all playable in your browser without an account.

A geography quiz trains a different kind of memory depending on what it asks. Recognising a flag is visual: you learn colours and patterns. Recalling a capital is associative: one name calls up another. Identifying a silhouette forces you to picture the map, and guessing a country from clues means reasoning about distances, populations and neighbours. The story mode campaign mixes all of it over the long run.

That is why GeoG offers five quizzes rather than one, sharing the same list of countries and the same 0 to 1000 scoring. If you want to really improve, alternate: flags and capitals for memory, silhouettes and Guess the Country for your mental map. Newcomers can start with the campaign, whose difficulty follows how well-known each country is and never jumps all at once.

Each section below covers one mode: how it works, what makes it hard, and how to approach it. The button in each section opens that mode straight in the game.

Flag game: recognise all {{countries}} flags of the world

A flag appears, four countries are offered, and you have seconds to choose. The mode covers all {{countries}} countries in the game — not just the thirty everybody knows.

The principle fits in a sentence: a flag appears, you name the country among the options. What makes the difference is how the questions are drawn.

GeoG ranks all {{countries}} countries by familiarity — an internal measure of how often a country is known to the general public. So a run does not just pick at random: it mixes obvious flags with rare ones, so that a session stays playable without ever becoming pure recitation. The further you go, the larger the share of obscure countries.

Speed and accuracy both count towards the score, normalised on a 0–1000 scale shared by every mode in the game. Answering correctly in two seconds is worth more than answering correctly in ten.

Three ways to play it: solo, picking a continent to target one area; in the daily challenge, where every player faces the exact same run; or in an online duel, against a friend or a matched opponent.

World capitals quiz

A country appears, four capitals are offered. The mode covers all {{countries}} countries in the game, including the ones whose capital is not the city you would expect.

A country appears, you pick its capital from four options. The wrong answers are not random: most often they are the country's largest city, or a neighbour's capital. That is what makes the mode instructive rather than mechanical — it goes straight at the confusions people actually hold.

The score depends on correctness and response time, on a 0–1000 scale shared by every mode. A run of quick answers pushes the total up faster than a series of laboured correct ones.

As with flags, a solo run can be limited to one continent. That is the most efficient way to handle a blind spot: almost everyone knows Europe and almost nobody knows the Caribbean.

The mode also feeds the daily challenge and online duels, where both players get exactly the same questions.

Guess the country by its shape

A black shape on parchment, with no name, no neighbouring borders and no scale. It is the toughest mode in the game — and the one that teaches fastest.

A country's outline appears, on its own: no name, no bordering countries, no landmark. All that is left is the contour.

What makes the mode distinctive is that you choose your difficulty on every question:

  • DUO — two options, 1 point;
  • CARRÉ — four options, 3 points;
  • CASH — nothing at all, you type the name, 5 points.

So you can play it safe on the shapes you hesitate over, and go for CASH on the ones you are sure of. That is what keeps the mode playable for a beginner without making it trivial for someone who knows their maps.

One tip that changes everything: the wrong options nearly always come from the same continent. When a shape suggests West Africa, do not stop at ruling out Asia — look at the coastlines, the peninsulas, the attached islands. Those are what separate Guinea from Ghana.

A note on method: the game's silhouettes are cropped to the main territory. France is metropolitan here, without French Guiana or the other distant territories — otherwise almost every outline would become illegible.

Guess the country from its clues

A country is hidden. With every guess the game tells you how warm you are: right continent, distance as the crow flies, direction, population gap. Your job is to close in.

A mystery country is drawn. You name a country, and the game compares. No vague hot-or-cold: each guess returns precise clues.

  • The continent — right or wrong, the clue that eliminates fastest;
  • The direction to travel from your guess, and the distance as the crow flies;
  • Population and area, larger or smaller than the target;
  • GDP per capita and life expectancy, which often separate two neighbours;
  • Whether it has a coastline, and its number of land borders.

Nine clues in all, recomputed on every attempt. None of them gives the answer alone; it is crossing them that does.

The right strategy is not to guess wildly but to grid-search: start with one country per continent to find the right one, then use direction and distance to close in, and finally population to separate the last candidates. It is exactly the reasoning you apply without thinking when reading a map — the mode makes it explicit.

The fewer guesses you use, the more you score. A player who knows their subregions finds most countries in four or five.

Story mode: {{storyLevels}} levels around the world

A map, {{storyLevels}} levels, three stars to earn on each. The difficulty does not rise at random: it follows how familiar each country is, from the obvious to the forgotten.

Story mode is a campaign of {{storyLevels}} levels laid out on a map you climb level by level. Each level offers a handful of questions drawn from the game's modes, and earns one to three stars depending on your score. You need a minimum of stars to continue, never perfection.

Difficulty that follows familiarity, not chance

That is what sets the mode apart from a mere sequence of games. GeoG ranks its {{countries}} countries by familiarity, and the campaign follows that scale: the first levels draw from countries everyone knows, the last from countries almost nobody can place. Progression is steady, with a wide enough band that each level stays varied.

In practice, level 1 will ask you for France or Brazil; level {{storyLevels}}, for Kiribati or Turkmenistan.

Lives, and ways to get them back

Failing a level costs a life. Lives regenerate over time, and you can recover one immediately — it is the only place in the game where an advert is offered, never imposed.

Offline

Story mode works without a connection in the mobile app: the entire campaign is derived from the level number, with no network call. It is the format to take on a plane or the underground.

Difficulty, continent by continent

Frequently asked

How many flags are in the game?
{{countries}} — the 193 UN member states plus the Holy See and Palestine. The reasoning is set out in our guide on how many countries there are.
Is the flag game free?
Entirely, and no account is needed for the daily challenge. It runs in the browser, or through the iPhone and Android app.
Can I practise one continent at a time?
Yes. Solo play lets you pick a zone: Europe, Africa, Asia, North America, South America or Oceania. It is the fastest way to fill a specific gap.
Which flags are hardest?
Those of the Oceanian and Caribbean micro-states, and the lookalike pairs — Chad and Romania, Monaco and Indonesia, Australia and New Zealand.
What is the capital of Australia?
Canberra, not Sydney. The city was built between Sydney and Melbourne, which were fighting over the title in 1908. It is the most common trap in the mode.
How many capitals does the quiz cover?
{{countries}}, one per country in the game. States with multiple or contested capitals follow atlas convention: Pretoria for South Africa, Sucre for Bolivia, Amsterdam for the Netherlands.
Can I play without an account?
Yes. The daily challenge starts straight in the browser. A free account is only for keeping your progress and playing online.
Can I revise one continent?
Yes: solo play lets you restrict a run to a single continent.
How do you recognise a country from its shape?
By looking first at coastlines, peninsulas and attached islands: they are the most distinctive features. Compactness helps too — a landlocked country has no coastline, so a continuous and often more regular contour.
What do DUO, CARRÉ and CASH mean?
They are the three difficulty levels you pick on each question: DUO gives two options (1 point), CARRÉ four (3 points), CASH none at all and asks you to type the name (5 points).
Why does France not include French Guiana?
Silhouettes are cropped to the main territory. Without that, a country with distant territories would produce an illegible outline at screen scale. Borders mode uses the same convention.
What is the best strategy?
Start with one country per continent to identify the right one, then use direction and distance to close in, and population to separate the last candidates.
How many guesses does it usually take?
A player who knows the subregions usually gets there in four or five. The score depends directly on the number of attempts.
Are the clues always the same?
Yes: continent, direction, distance, population, area, GDP per capita, life expectancy, coastline and number of land borders. What changes is the hidden country — and in the daily challenge it is the same one for every player in the world.
How many levels does story mode have?
{{storyLevels}}, from the best known to the most obscure. Difficulty follows how familiar the countries are, not a random draw.
Is story mode free?
Entirely. Lives regenerate over time; an optional advert lets you get one back immediately, but it is never imposed.
Can I play offline?
Yes, in the mobile app: the whole campaign is derived from the level number, with no network call.
Do I need three stars to move on?
No. A minimum threshold unlocks the next level; the three stars are there for players who want to finish everything perfectly.
