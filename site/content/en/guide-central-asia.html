Central Asia on the globe: placing the five “-stans”

Five landlocked countries, look-alike names, and a flat map that squashes them. On a globe, three landmarks are enough to sort them out.

Guide · game data, November 2026

Central Asia is one of the areas where players lose the most points on a blank map. The game files five countries there, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan, all with a name ending in “-stan” and none with a coastline. Their names are alike, their capitals unfamiliar, and on a flat world map they fit in a corner. The 3D Globe mode changes things: the region keeps its true size, and its neighbours, Russia, China, Iran and Afghanistan, are all visible around it. Here is how to cut it up.

First observation: the gaps are huge. Kazakhstan covers more than 2.7 million km², a little over twice the combined area of the other four (about 1.28 million km²), yet it has only about 8 inhabitants per square kilometre. Uzbekistan, six times smaller, is home on its own to about 44% of the five countries’ 82 million people, with a density close to 81 inhabitants per square kilometre.

Three landmarks to place them

1. Kazakhstan, the giant of the north

It is the only one that touches both Russia, to the north, and China, to the east, and the largest landlocked country in the world. It closes the region from above and borders the Caspian Sea to the west. Its capital, Astana, replaced Almaty in 1997; the city was named Nur-Sultan from 2019 to 2022 before taking back the name Astana, which the game uses (see the guide to countries that changed name). Almaty, further south-east, remains the largest city and the reflex answer to avoid.

2. The south-west trio: Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, and what links them

In the south-west lie Turkmenistan, which borders the Caspian, Iran and Afghanistan, and Uzbekistan, at the exact centre of the region. Uzbekistan is the only one of the five to touch all four others: it has five neighbours in the game (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Turkmenistan), and every one of them is landlocked too. That makes it a doubly landlocked country, a rarity covered in the guide to landlocked countries. Capitals: Tashkent for Uzbekistan, Ashgabat for Turkmenistan.

3. The mountain pair in the east

Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan occupy the high ground in the east, against China. Kyrgyzstan is in the north, Tajikistan in the south, and the latter also touches Afghanistan. Their capitals are Bishkek and Dushanbe: the simplest memory aid is to take them in north-to-south order, like the countries, with B for Bishkek before D for Dushanbe, alphabetically as well.

The neighbours that frame the region

To avoid drifting, lean on the big neighbours: Russia to the north, China to the east, Afghanistan and Iran to the south. Turkmenistan is the only one of the five to touch Iran, and Tajikistan and Uzbekistan are the only ones, with Turkmenistan, to touch Afghanistan. China touches only three of the five: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan. An effective globe exercise: put a finger on China, then name the three countries that touch it on the Central Asian side, from north to south: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan.

A two-session revision plan

First session: place without names. Spin the globe over the region and simply touch the “big country in the north” and the “small country in the middle” before worrying about names. Second session: launch the 3D Globe mode choosing Asia, which gathers these countries with their Asian neighbours, and redo a ten-question run, noting the ones where you mix up Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan, the pair that swaps places in memory most often.

Frequently asked questions

Which are the five Central Asian countries?
Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan. In the game all five are filed under Central Asia.
What is the capital of Kazakhstan?
Astana, which replaced Almaty as capital in 1997. The city was called Nur-Sultan from 2019 to 2022.
What is a doubly landlocked country?
A country with no sea access whose neighbours are all landlocked too. Uzbekistan is the Central Asian example.
How can I stop mixing up Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan?
Kyrgyzstan is in the north, Tajikistan in the south, and it is Tajikistan that touches Afghanistan. Their capitals follow the same order: Bishkek, then Dushanbe.
