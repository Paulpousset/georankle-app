Malaysia

A country in two pieces, separated by the South China Sea.

Country profile · game data, October 2026

Malaysia has 35.6 million people according to the game, forty-fourth in the world. Its 330,411 km² make it sixty-seventh by area, with about 108 people per square kilometre. For a Southeast Asian country that is a middling size, between Thailand, which is larger, and Vietnam, which has more people.

The capital is Kuala Lumpur, the largest city. The federal government sits in Putrajaya, a planned city built near Kuala Lumpur, which is a classic trap: the game keeps Kuala Lumpur, but a question about “the seat of government” could throw you. Asia has a similar case in Sri Lanka, and both are among the eleven capitals with a special status in our Asia capitals quiz.

What sets Malaysia apart on a map is its two-part shape. One part covers the south of the Malay Peninsula and touches Thailand to the north; the other lies on the big island of Borneo, shared with Brunei, which is enclosed within its land, and with Indonesia. The game therefore counts three land neighbours, spread over the two pieces. The South China Sea runs between them. At the southern tip of the peninsula, Singapore does not appear in that list: the city-state is separated by a narrow strait.

The flag looks like the American one: horizontal red and white stripes and a blue canton at the top left, but with a yellow crescent and star in place of white stars. The yellow crescent and the star with fourteen points, like the fourteen stripes, are the surest way not to mix it up with the US flag.
