Rankle: ranking countries by a criterion

Eight countries, eight criteria, one slot each. For every country you have to find where it shines — not where it is good, where it is best.

How it works

Rankle is GeoG's original mode, and the only one that needs no map knowledge at all. Eight countries are shown one at a time, against eight ranking criteria: population, area, GDP, coastline length, alcohol consumption, passport power…

For each country you pick the criterion where its world rank is closest to first place. Each criterion is used only once, so you have to spread eight countries across eight slots — which turns the round into an assignment problem rather than a sequence of independent questions.

At the end your total is compared to the optimal placement — the best possible arrangement for those eight countries. The score is expressed as an efficiency percentage: 100 % means no other combination could have done better.

That is where the difficulty lies. Putting Russia on “area” looks obvious, until you realise no other country in the round could have taken that slot, and you should have saved it for last. Rankle rewards seeing the whole board.

Two variants come from it: Streak, where a single country appears with four criteria and one mistake ends the run, and Higher or Lower, which compares two countries on one criterion.

Frequently asked

How do you score in Rankle?
By placing each country on the criterion where its world rank is closest to first place. The total is then compared to the optimal placement and shown as an efficiency percentage.
How many criteria are there?
{{themes}} in total, from population to the number of UNESCO World Heritage sites. Each round draws eight of them.
Where does the data come from?
Public sources: the World Bank, the United Nations, UNESCO, international sports federations. Each criterion states its source in the game.
How is it different from Streak mode?
Streak shows one country at a time with four criteria, and the run ends on the first mistake. Rankle asks you to spread eight countries across eight criteria in a single grid.

Daily, online, on mobile

Daily Rankle. Rankle and Streak are part of the daily challenge: at midnight UTC, the same grid for every player, a single attempt, and a result grid to share without giving the answers away. Free Rankle draws a new grid every game.

Against someone. A Rankle grid is worth 0 to 1000 points like any round (1 % efficiency = 10 points): it can be a duel round, a ranked run or a league challenge. Both players get the same grid; a free account is all it takes.

Where to play. In the browser, nothing to install, or in the GeoG app on iPhone and Android — same {{themes}} criteria, same account, same progress.
