Oceania capitals quiz: 14 countries, 4 blocks and the capitals that are not the big city

Fourteen capitals, twelve of which almost nobody can place. The good news: they sort into four blocks, and a single rule covers most of the traps.

Guide · game data, November 2026

The game counts {{count:oceanie}} countries in Oceania. The full list, with the story of Canberra, Wellington and the atoll capitals, is in the atlas of Oceania’s capitals. This page is for learning them: it splits the continent along the four subregions of the game data, singles out the capitals that are not their country’s largest city, and suggests a revision order that fits in two sessions.

A continent of three countries, then eleven more

A sense of scale first. According to the game data, Oceania holds a little under 46 million people, and three countries account for 94 % of them: Australia (59 %), Papua New Guinea (23 %) and New Zealand (12 %). The other eleven countries total 2.7 million people on 65,150 km², less than Lithuania or Sri Lanka in area. Australia alone covers 91 % of the continent’s land. Another oddity: a single land border on the whole continent, Papua New Guinea’s with Indonesia. The other thirteen countries have no playable neighbour in Borders mode.

For a quiz, that means a player who knows Canberra, Port Moresby and Wellington has covered 94 % of the population but only three questions out of fourteen. The remaining eleven capitals weigh as much in a draw as the eleven largest countries in the world. That is the whole point of this page.

Four blocks taken from the data

Each Oceanian country carries a subregion in the game data, the same one Guess the Country uses for its first clue. It yields four blocks, listed here by number of countries.

{{table:capital-blocks:oceanie}}

Australia and New Zealand: two countries

The only two capitals everyone has already heard of, and yet two of the game’s most classic traps: neither Sydney nor Auckland. They take a minute to revise and stick for good.

Melanesia: four countries, two “Ports”

Papua New Guinea, Fiji, the Solomon Islands and Vanuatu form the arc closest to Australia, and the most populous of the three island blocks. Two of its capitals start with “Port”: Port Moresby for Papua New Guinea, Port Vila for Vanuatu. The other two are Suva (Fiji) and Honiara (Solomon Islands). No other capital on the continent starts with “Port”: if a question offers a “Port”, the answer is in Melanesia.

Micronesia: five countries, the hard block

Kiribati, the Federated States of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands, Palau and Nauru are the five least-known countries of the continent, and probably of the game. Three difficulties add up. Micronesia is both a region, which contains all five, and a country, whose capital is Palikir. Kiribati is pronounced “Kiribass”, and its capital, South Tarawa, is the only capital in the game whose name contains a compass point. Finally Ngerulmud, the capital of Palau, is the newest on the continent and one of the least populous in the world; Majuro (Marshall Islands) and Yaren (Nauru) complete the block.

Polynesia: three countries

Samoa, Tonga and Tuvalu are the game’s three Polynesian countries. Apia, Nuku’alofa and Funafuti are best learnt together: three names ending in “a” for three countries, two of which, Tonga and Tuvalu, are among the fifteen least populous in the world.

The capitals that are not the largest city

The rule that costs the most points on other continents, the famous big city that is not the capital, applies here five times out of fourteen.

  • Canberra, not Sydney or Melbourne, for Australia.
  • Wellington, not Auckland, for New Zealand.
  • Palikir, not Weno, for the Federated States of Micronesia: the capital is on the island of Pohnpei, the most populous town in the state of Chuuk.
  • Ngerulmud, not Koror, for Palau: Koror was the capital until 2006 and remains by far the main town.
  • Yaren for Nauru, which officially has no capital: it is the district where parliament sits, kept by convention in the game as in atlases.

The other nine capitals are also their country’s largest city, which makes them easier: Port Moresby, Suva, Honiara, Port Vila, South Tarawa, Majuro, Apia, Nuku’alofa and Funafuti. In practice, when a question offers a well-known Oceanian city that is not on the list of fourteen, it almost always comes from one of the five countries above.

A two-session revision plan

  1. Session 1: Australia, New Zealand, Melanesia and Polynesia. Nine countries. Start with Canberra and Wellington, add the two “Ports” then Suva and Honiara, and finish with the three Polynesian names in “a”.
  2. Session 2: Micronesia. Five countries, the block that needs the most attention. Learn the region-country pair first (Micronesia, Palikir), then South Tarawa and Majuro, and keep Ngerulmud and Yaren for last. Then run a mixed series of all fourteen.

Two sessions two or three days apart are enough, at the pace suggested in the revision guide. The next step is to place these countries on the map, which is another matter: the guide to the hardest countries to locate explains why ten of the thirty least familiar countries are in Oceania, and the one on the smallest countries in the world shows that eleven of the continent’s fourteen countries have fewer than one million inhabitants.

Frequently asked

How many capitals does Oceania have in the game?
{{count:oceanie}}, one per country. Non-sovereign territories such as New Caledonia, French Polynesia, Guam or Hawaii are not countries in the game, so there is no capital to learn for them here.
What is the capital of Micronesia?
Palikir, on the island of Pohnpei. Mind the double meaning: Micronesia is also the region that groups Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Palau and Nauru.
What is the capital of Kiribati?
South Tarawa, on Tarawa atoll. It is the only capital in the game whose name contains a compass point.
How many Oceanian countries have a land border?
Just one: Papua New Guinea, which shares the island of New Guinea with Indonesia. The other thirteen are islands or archipelagos with no land neighbour.
