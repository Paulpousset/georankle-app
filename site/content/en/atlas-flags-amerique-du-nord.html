The flags of North America

Three flags everyone knows, seven that look alike, and thirteen nobody revises. That is where the quiz is decided.

Reference · {{count:amerique-du-nord}} flags · game data, updated August 2026

“North America” is taken here in the broad sense: Canada, the United States and Mexico, but also Central America and the Caribbean — {{count:amerique-du-nord}} countries. That convention is the one used in French-language teaching, and it is what makes this page useful: the thirteen island states are precisely the ones people forget.

The Central American block: blue, white, blue

Five Central American countries carry three horizontal blue-white-blue bands: Guatemala (vertical bands), El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua — and you can add Argentina further south. It is no coincidence: all descend from the flag of the United Provinces of Central America, a short-lived federation from 1823 to 1841.

Telling them apart comes down to the centre: an elaborate coat of arms for Guatemala and El Salvador, five stars in an arc for Honduras, a triangle with a rainbow for Nicaragua.

The Caribbean, where points are lost

Caribbean flags are visually the most inventive in the world: the diagonals of Trinidad and Tobago, Jamaica and Saint Kitts and Nevis, the nested triangles of Saint Lucia and Dominica — the latter being the only flag in the world containing purple, thanks to its sisserou parrot.

Their difficulty is not graphic, it is statistical: in a random draw, Grenada carries exactly the same weight as the United States. Learning them as a block is the best return on the whole continent.

The Mexican case

Mexico and Italy share the same three green, white and red bands. The central coat of arms — an eagle devouring a snake on a cactus — separates them instantly, but at thumbnail size the confusion happens more often than you would think.

Frequently asked

Why do Central American flags look alike?
They all descend from the flag of the United Provinces of Central America (1823–1841): blue, white, blue. Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua inherited it.
Which is the only flag in the world with purple?
Dominica’s, thanks to the sisserou parrot at its centre. No other national flag uses that colour.
How do you tell Mexico from Italy?
By the central emblem: Mexico carries an eagle devouring a snake on a cactus, Italy has none. The proportions differ as well.
