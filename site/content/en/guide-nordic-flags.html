Nordic flags: the Scandinavian cross, five countries, one rule

Five flags built on the same design, a cross shifted towards the hoist. One question about colour is enough to separate them.

Guide · game data, October 2026

Among the game’s {{countries}} flags, five form a family that even a beginner spots from afar: Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland. All carry a cross shifted towards the hoist, its horizontal arm running on from the short vertical one. That is the “Scandinavian cross”, and it explains both why these flags are instantly recognised as Nordic and why players swap them. This page does not repeat the list of similar flags: it takes a whole family and gives a method to sort it.

The five flags and their countries

The five countries sit together in the game’s Northern Europe block, which also makes this the smallest coherent series you can learn in one go. Their geography is less tidy: only Norway, Sweden and Finland touch one another, in a chain, with Norway also sharing a border with Russia. Denmark has a single land neighbour in the game, Germany, and Iceland, an island in the North Atlantic, has none.

The rule: look at the field first

The cross is drawn the same way everywhere, so it is the colour of the field that sorts the five flags into groups.

  • Red field: Denmark, with a white cross, and Norway, with a blue cross edged in white. The Danish flag is the simplest, two colours; the Norwegian adds a third.
  • Blue field: Sweden, with a yellow cross, and Iceland, with a white cross edged in red.
  • White field: Finland, with a blue cross. It is the only one of the five with a light field, so the easiest to isolate.

That leaves the two pairs to separate. Two details are enough: how many colours and which colour in the centre. If the flag has only two colours, it is Denmark (red and white), Sweden (blue and yellow) or Finland (white and blue). If it has three, it is Norway or Iceland, which are nearly a negative image of each other: Norway has a red field and a blue cross, Iceland a blue field and a red cross, each outlined in white. One sentence keeps the pair straight: “Norway is red outside, Iceland blue outside.”

Where does the design come from?

The model is Denmark’s flag, the Dannebrog. By tradition it fell from the sky during a battle in 1219, and it is one of the oldest national flags still in use. The other Nordic countries borrowed it and changed the colours, claiming an identity of their own while keeping a family tie. Norway, long united with Denmark, adopted in 1821 a flag with a blue cross on the Danish red field. Iceland, tied to the Danish crown until 1944, settled on its own at independence. Sweden took the blue and yellow of its coat of arms, and Finland chose in 1918 the white of its snow and the blue of its lakes, in the usual interpretation.

Not to be confused with

Outside the family, three flags play impostor. Switzerland is red with a white cross, but its cross is square and centred, with equal arms: it is never a Scandinavian cross. The Ukrainian flag, blue above and yellow below, borrows Sweden’s colours without a cross, and is often mistaken for it (see the Ukraine profile). Finally, Greece mixes blue and white, but with horizontal stripes and a small cross in the corner.

Territories that also fly a Nordic cross, such as the Faroe Islands or Åland, are not countries in the game: only the five sovereign states need to be known for a quiz.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Scandinavian cross?
It is a cross whose vertical arm is shifted towards the hoist, with horizontal arms reaching the edges of the flag. It comes from the Danish flag and appears on five national flags in the game.
Which countries have a Nordic-cross flag?
Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland. They are the five Nordic countries in the game.
How do you tell Norway from Iceland?
Both use red, white and blue. Norway has a red field and a blue cross, Iceland a blue field and a red cross; in both, the cross is edged in white.
Why does Sweden’s flag look like Ukraine’s?
Both use blue and yellow. Sweden has a yellow cross on a blue field, Ukraine two horizontal bands, blue above and yellow below.
