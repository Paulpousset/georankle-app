Ghana

The densest country in this stretch of the ranking, and the model for a whole family of African flags.

Country profile · game data, October 2026

Ghana has 34.4 million people according to the game, forty-seventh in the world, on 238,539 km², the eightieth largest territory. That works out at about 144 people per square kilometre, a high density for West Africa, and the reason this “small” country weighs almost as much as Angola, which is more than five times larger.

The capital is Accra, on the Gulf of Guinea, and also the largest city. The country was called the Gold Coast before independence in 1957, the first for a sub-Saharan country. The date matters for the flag: by choosing Ethiopia’s red, gold and green and adding a black star, Ghana became a model for many African states that gained independence after it.

The flag has three horizontal bands, red, gold and green, with a black five-pointed star in the centre. The trap is its resemblance to Mali, Senegal, Guinea or Cameroon, which reuse the same colours. The rule to keep: horizontal bands and a black star mean Ghana. The others have vertical bands. The full comparison is in African flag families.

The game lists three land neighbours: Togo to the east, Burkina Faso to the north and Ivory Coast to the west. One map curiosity: the Greenwich meridian crosses the country, which makes it one of the few states straddling longitude zero. As for language, English dominates, which sets Ghana apart from its French-speaking neighbours.
