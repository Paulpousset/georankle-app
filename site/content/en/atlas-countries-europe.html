The countries of Europe

Forty-five states on the smallest inhabited continent, and a size gap that exists nowhere else: between Russia and the Vatican the ratio is one to thirty-five million.

Reference · {{count:europe}} countries · game data, updated August 2026

Europe has {{count:europe}} countries in the count used here — the {{countries}} states GeoG recognises, meaning the 193 UN members and its two observers. Russia is included, because that is where most of its population lives and where its capital is; Turkey and Kazakhstan are filed under Asia.

The continent of size extremes

No other continent spreads so wide. Russia is the largest country in the world; Vatican City, at under half a square kilometre and fewer than a thousand inhabitants, is the smallest sovereign state on the planet. Between them, Europe lines up four more micro-states — Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein and Andorra — which in a random draw weigh exactly as much as Germany.

Those five are the classic blind spot of revision. They have their own guide: the micro-states.

Fourteen landlocked countries

Europe has fourteen states with no access to the sea, nearly a third of the continent: Austria, Switzerland, Hungary, Slovakia, Czechia, Serbia, North Macedonia, Moldova, Belarus, Luxembourg, and the four Alpine and Pyrenean micro-states. One of them, Liechtenstein, is doubly landlocked: both its neighbours — Switzerland and Austria — have no coastline either. There is only one other such case in the world, Uzbekistan.

The most surrounded country

Russia shares a land border with fourteen countries, tied worldwide with China. Germany has nine, and France eight in the game's reckoning, which keeps only metropolitan territory.
