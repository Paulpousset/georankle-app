African flag families: red-gold-green, North Africa, look-alikes and one-offs

Of the {{count:afrique}} flags of Africa, thirty-five fall into a handful of families. Knowing which family a flag belongs to divides the work by four.

Guide · game data, October 2026

The page flags of Africa shows all {{count:afrique}} flags in alphabetical order. This page works the other way round: instead of reading a list, it groups flags by resemblance, the way the eye does when you play. The four families below cover thirty-five countries; the other nineteen, often simple striped flags like Nigeria’s, are best recognised through their capital, which the table in the illustrated guide gives next to each flag.

Family 1: red, gold and green

This is the largest family and the most misleading. The three colours come from Ethiopia, a country whose resistance kept it from lasting colonisation. At independence Ghana (1957) took them up with a black star, and many African states did the same. Thirteen flags in the game are built on this trio:

{{table:flag-set:africa-red-gold-green}}

The trio is therefore never enough: look at the orientation and the symbol. Mali and Guinea use exactly the same three vertical bands in opposite order: green-gold-red for Mali, red-gold-green for Guinea. Senegal copies Mali’s layout with a green star in the centre. Ghana has its bands horizontal, with a black star. Cameroon combines three vertical bands of green, red and gold with a gold star. Benin and Togo break the pattern, with a vertical band or a separate canton.

Family 2: the north, red, white, black and green

North of the Sahara the palettes are those of Arab flags: red, white, black and green, often with a crescent. Six countries in the game belong here:

{{table:flag-set:africa-pan-arab}}

Egypt and Sudan share three bands of red, white and black; Egypt adds a golden eagle in the centre, Sudan a green triangle at the hoist. Algeria and Tunisia each have a red crescent and star, one on green and white, the other on a white disc. Libya, red, black and green, carries a white crescent. Morocco is the only one with a single field colour, red, with a green pentagram.

Family 3: those that recall another flag, and sky blue

Here the trap is not inside Africa but outside. Four flags recall a flag from another continent, and two others stand out for a colour that is rare in Africa:

{{table:flag-set:africa-borrowed}}
  • Liberia: eleven red and white stripes and a star in a blue canton, the outline of the American flag. The country was founded in the nineteenth century by freed slaves from the United States.
  • Chad: three vertical bands of blue, yellow and red, nearly identical to Romania’s.
  • Ivory Coast: orange, white, green, which is the Irish flag read backwards.
  • Niger: orange, white, green, with an orange disc in the centre. India has the same shades with a blue wheel.
  • Somalia and Djibouti: sky blue is rare on the continent (Botswana, in the next family, is the third). Somalia places a white star on it, Djibouti a white triangle with a red star on a blue and green field.

Family 4: ten flags with no equivalent

This is the best news for a quiz: ten flags look like nothing else.

{{table:flag-set:africa-unique}}

South Africa has six colours and a sideways Y, adopted in 1994. Mozambique carries an assault rifle on its emblem, which is unique among national flags. Kenya has a Maasai shield and two spears. Botswana is sky blue with a black band. Uganda alternates six black, yellow and red bands around a crowned crane. Malawi has a rising sun on its black band. Zambia has an eagle on a green field and three bands of red, black and orange. Namibia is cut by a red diagonal. Angola combines a cogwheel and a machete. Eritrea has a red triangle and an olive wreath.

A three-question method

  1. Are there red, gold and green together? If so, compare the orientation of the bands, then look for the star.
  2. Is there a crescent, or red-white-black? Then it is the north, and only six candidates remain.
  3. A figurative symbol (animal, weapon, sun)? It is almost always one of the ten flags with no equivalent.

Frequently asked questions

Why are so many African flags red, gold and green?
They are the colours of Ethiopia, adopted by many states at independence as a sign of African unity. Thirteen flags in the game are built on this trio.
What is the difference between the flags of Mali and Guinea?
Both have three vertical bands of green, gold and red, but in opposite order: green-gold-red for Mali, red-gold-green for Guinea.
Which African flag looks like the American flag?
Liberia’s: eleven red and white stripes and a white star in a blue canton. The country was founded by freed American slaves.
Which African flags resemble flags from other continents?
Chad’s resembles Romania’s, Ivory Coast’s is Ireland’s reversed and Niger’s echoes India’s. Liberia’s recalls the American flag.
