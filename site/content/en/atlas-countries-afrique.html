The countries of Africa

Fifty-four states, more than a quarter of the world’s countries, and sixteen of them with no access to the sea at all. No continent is this fragmented.

Reference · {{count:afrique}} countries · game data, updated August 2026

With {{count:afrique}} countries out of {{countries}}, Africa is the most fragmented continent on the planet. Part of that fragmentation dates back to the Berlin Conference of 1884–85, where borders were drawn with a ruler with no regard for peoples — which is also why so many African borders are straight lines.

Sixteen landlocked countries

That is the world record. Mali, Niger, Chad, Burkina Faso, the Central African Republic, South Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Lesotho and Eswatini have no access to the sea. Two of them are even enclosed within a single other country: Lesotho, entirely surrounded by South Africa, and — outside the continent — San Marino and the Vatican inside Italy.

Ethiopia is a case apart: it had a coastline until 1993 and Eritrean independence. It is now the most populous landlocked country in the world.

Dizzying population gaps

Nigeria approaches 233 million inhabitants — more than the twenty-five least populous African countries combined. At the other end, the Seychelles hold about 121,000 people, the population of a mid-sized town. Africa combines the fastest-growing populations on Earth with a dozen tiny island states in the Indian and Atlantic oceans.

The most surrounded country

The Democratic Republic of the Congo shares a border with nine countries, making it the African champion — and the second-largest state on the continent after Algeria.

Which is the most populous landlocked African country?
Ethiopia, with around 132 million inhabitants. It lost its access to the sea in 1993, when Eritrea became independent.
Which country is entirely surrounded by a single other?
Lesotho, enclosed within South Africa. The two other cases in the world are San Marino and Vatican City, both surrounded by Italy.
