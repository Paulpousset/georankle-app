Zimbabwe

A landlocked plateau between the Zambezi and the Limpopo, whose northern border is drawn by one of Africa’s most famous rivers.

Country profile · game data, November 2026

Zimbabwe has 16.6 million inhabitants according to the game data, ranking 73rd in the world and 26th in Africa. Its territory, 390,760 km², holds the 61st rank by area, a little more than Germany or Japan, for a density of about 43 people per square kilometre.

The capital is Harare, in the north-east. The city was called Salisbury until 1982, two years after independence; the country itself was named Southern Rhodesia. Bulawayo, the second city, is in the south-west and causes no confusion in a quiz.

The country is landlocked and has four neighbours: Zambia to the north, Mozambique to the east, South Africa to the south and Botswana to the west. Two of those borders are rivers. The Zambezi separates Zimbabwe from Zambia, and it is on that stretch that the Victoria Falls lie, shared between the two countries. The Limpopo marks the limit with South Africa. To place the country on the globe, look for the squarish block just north of South Africa, away from the coast.

The flag is one of the busiest on the continent: seven horizontal stripes (green, yellow, red, black, red, yellow, green) and, at the hoist, a white triangle edged in black bearing the Zimbabwe Bird, a carving found at the Great Zimbabwe site, in front of a red star.
