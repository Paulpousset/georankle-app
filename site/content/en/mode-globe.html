How it works

A country name appears, you rotate the globe and tap the right territory. One finger to spin, two to zoom; on a desktop, the mouse and wheel do the same.

Choosing a sphere over a map is not decorative. Every flat projection distorts: the most common one, Mercator, inflates high latitudes so much that Greenland looks as large as Africa, when it is fourteen times smaller. A globe does not lie about area, and it teaches real relative positions along the way — that Norway sits at the latitude of Alaska, that Sicily is further south than Tunis.

Micro-states are handled: Monaco, San Marino, the Vatican, Nauru, Tuvalu and a few others are too small to tap at globe scale, so they appear as a tappable dot as soon as you zoom in far enough.

The score combines accuracy and time. As everywhere in the game, a solo run can be limited to one continent.

Frequently asked

Why a globe rather than a map?
Because every flat map distorts. The Mercator projection makes Greenland look as big as Africa when it is fourteen times smaller. A globe gives real areas and real relative positions.
Can you tap the tiny countries?
Yes. Monaco, San Marino, the Vatican, Nauru, Tuvalu and the other micro-states appear as tappable dots once you zoom in close enough.
Does the globe work in a browser?
Yes, with no install and no plugin. It also works offline in the mobile app.
Can I practise one continent?
Yes: solo play lets you restrict the run to Europe, Africa, Asia, North America, South America or Oceania.
