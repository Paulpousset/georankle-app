Rwanda

The land of a thousand hills fits on a territory smaller than Belgium and yet shelters more than 14 million people.

Country profile · game data, November 2026

According to the game data, Rwanda has 14.3 million inhabitants, ranking it 76th in the world and 29th in Africa. Its area, 26,338 km², only reaches the 144th rank: it is the ninth-smallest country on the continent. The result is a density of about 541 people per square kilometre, the second-highest in Africa after Mauritius and one of the highest in the world for a country that is neither an island nor a city-state.

The capital, Kigali, sits at the exact centre of the country, which is rare in Africa, where capitals are often coastal. There is no rival city to remember: Kigali is the answer, and by far the largest city.

Rwanda is landlocked, with four neighbours: Uganda to the north, Tanzania to the east, Burundi to the south and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west, across Lake Kivu. That lake, stretched from north to south, forms almost the whole western border and gives the country its most visible landmark on the globe. Rwanda and Burundi are so alike in size, shape and population that they are best revised together: Rwanda is the northern one.

The flag, adopted in 2001, consists of three horizontal bands, blue, yellow and green, with a yellow sun of twenty-four rays in the upper right corner. It replaced a red, yellow and green tricolour that bore the letter R.
