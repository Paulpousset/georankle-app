The flags of Africa

Fifty-four flags, two dominant palettes, and a geography you can read in the colours: an Arab north, a pan-African rest.

Reference · {{count:afrique}} flags · game data, updated August 2026

It is the continent with the most countries — {{count:afrique}} of the world's {{countries}}, more than a quarter — and yet one of the most legible, because its flags fall into two great families separated by the Sahara.

Red, yellow and green

These are the colours of Ethiopia, the only African country never durably colonised. At independence, in the 1950s and 1960s, some thirty states adopted them as a sign of African continuity: Ghana, Senegal, Mali, Guinea, Cameroon, Benin, Togo, Congo, Zimbabwe.

That is the trap: when half a continent shares three colours, the arrangement and the central symbol give the answer. Vertical or horizontal bands, a black star for Ghana, a green star for Senegal, an AK-47 for Mozambique — the only national flag in the world depicting a modern firearm.

The pan-Arab north

North of the Sahara the palette changes: red, white, black and green, the pan-Arab colours from the flag of the 1916 Arab Revolt. Egypt, Sudan, Libya, but also Tunisia and Algeria with their crescent and star, and Morocco with its green pentagram on red.

The one-of-a-kind flags

A few flags resemble nothing else and are therefore the easiest to remember: the six colours of South Africa and its horizontal Y, the diamond of Eswatini with its shield, the spear of Kenya, the rising sun of Malawi, and the stylised zebra of Botswana — blue for water, black and white for racial harmony.

Frequently asked

Why are so many African flags red, yellow and green?
They are the pan-African colours, borrowed from Ethiopia — the only country on the continent never durably colonised. Around thirty countries adopted them at independence.
Which African flag shows a weapon?
Mozambique: its flag carries an AK-47 crossed with a hoe, over an open book. It is the only national flag in the world depicting a modern firearm.
