Benin

A strip of land stretched from the Gulf of Guinea to the Sahel, whose official capital is not the city where everything happens.

Country profile · game data, November 2026

Benin has 14.5 million inhabitants according to the game data, 75th in the world and 28th in Africa, on 114,760 km², the 101st territory on the list. At about 126 people per square kilometre, it is markedly denser than the continental average.

The capital kept by the game is Porto-Novo, the constitutional capital. The government, the port and most of the activity are nevertheless in Cotonou, the largest city, some thirty kilometres to the west. It is one of the most frequent mix-ups in the Africa capitals quiz, and Benin appears for that reason in the guide to countries with several capitals.

The country has four neighbours: Togo to the west, Nigeria to the east, Burkina Faso and Niger to the north. Its shape is the best way to locate it: a rectangle stretched from north to south, narrow on the coast and wider towards the Sahel, mirrored by Togo, thinner still, right next door. The Niger river forms part of the northern border. Until 1975 the country was called Dahomey, after the old kingdom of Abomey.

The flag combines a vertical green band at the hoist with two horizontal bands, yellow above red. These are the pan-African colours, but in a unique layout that avoids lookalikes, unlike its neighbours Mali or Guinea.
