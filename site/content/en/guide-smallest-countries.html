The smallest countries in the world: by area or by population?

“The smallest country in the world” has two answers depending on what you measure. They agree at the top, then the lists drift apart, and the trick questions hide in the gap.

Guide · game data, November 2026

The story of the micro-states, Vatican City, Monaco, Nauru or Tuvalu, is told in the micro-states guide. This page asks a different question: small compared with what? A country can fit on an atoll and still hold more people than a regional capital; another can spread over an island the size of a mid-sized European country and shelter only a few hundred thousand people. In a quiz both are “small”, but they are not revised the same way and they do not answer the same questions.

Two thresholds, two lists

Take two simple thresholds. Under 1,000 km², the game counts 25 countries. Under one million inhabitants, it counts 38. Only 23 meet both criteria at once. So two countries are tiny without being sparsely populated, and fifteen are sparsely populated without being tiny. In all, 40 countries are “small” one way or the other: more than one country in five in the game, for 0.24 % of the world’s population and 0.47 % of the land of the {{countries}} countries.

Sixteen countries are in bold: the two definitions agree on four fifths of the pack. The rest tells two opposite stories.

The crowded dwarfs

Four countries are among the twenty smallest by area but not among the twenty least populous: Barbados, the Maldives, Malta and Singapore. Singapore is the extreme case. The twentieth-smallest territory, 728 km² in the game data, the city-state is only the 84th least populous country, with a little over six million people, and by far the densest country on the list, at more than 8,000 people per square kilometre. Malta and the Maldives exceed 1,700 people per square kilometre; Bahrain, 23rd by area, approaches 2,000. For comparison, the average density of the game’s {{countries}} countries is about 58 people per square kilometre.

The consequence for a quiz: a question about “the smallest country” calls for the Vatican, but a question about “the densest country” calls for Singapore, and Monaco in most atlases. Those are two different records, held by two different countries.

The small countries that take up room

Conversely, four countries are among the twenty least populous without being among the twenty smallest: Dominica, Tonga, Kiribati and Samoa. The gap stays modest for the first three, which rank 21st, 22nd and 24th by area. It widens as soon as you go further down the list of the least populous.

{{table:least-populous:20}}

Right after these twenty come the real “small countries that take up room”. Iceland, the 24th least populous country with 386,506 inhabitants, covers 103,000 km², the 89th rank by area: bigger than Portugal, fewer people than Lyon. Suriname and Guyana, 32nd and 36th by population, are the two largest countries in the world with fewer than one million people, at 163,820 and 214,970 km² respectively. They are the only South American countries under that threshold, and their density, below four people per square kilometre, puts them among the least dense countries on the planet, alongside Mongolia, Canada and Australia.

Where the small countries are

The two lists do not share the same geography either. The 25 countries under 1,000 km² are spread between the Caribbean (7), Oceania (7), Europe (6), Asia (3) and Africa (2), with the Seychelles and São Tomé and Príncipe. South America has none. The 38 countries under one million people lean clearly towards the Pacific: eleven in Oceania, that is eleven of its fourteen countries, nine in Europe, nine in North America in the site’s broad sense, four in Africa, three in Asia and two in South America.

For revision, that gives two easy blocks to name. The “area” block is Caribbean and European: the six micro-states of Europe, the seven small Antilles from Antigua and Barbuda to Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. The “population” block is Oceanian: outside Australia, New Zealand and Papua New Guinea, every country in Oceania has fewer than one million inhabitants, and eight of them are among the twenty least populous in the world. The Oceania capitals quiz goes through that block capital by capital.

What it changes in the game

In Higher or Lower, the criterion on screen changes everything. On area, Iceland beats Belgium and Suriname beats South Korea; on population, both lose by a wide margin. A player who has filed Iceland under “small country” will be wrong half the time depending on the criterion drawn. In Guess the Country, the area clue and the population clue are read separately for the same reason: a small area with a large population points to an island or a city-state, a small population on a large territory points to Iceland, the Guianas, Mongolia or Namibia. In Rankle, finally, “area” and “population” are two distinct columns, and the Maldives or Malta are good countries to place on area, never on population.

A note on the data

The tables on this page are computed from the game data, so that they never contradict what the player sees in a round. Two figures deserve a word. The Vatican’s area is 0.49 km² there, where most sources give 0.44 km²: “under half a square kilometre” is true either way. Monaco’s, on the other hand, is 74.9 km² there, far above the roughly 2 km² usually quoted; with the usual value, Monaco would be second by area, right behind the Vatican, instead of fifth in the first table. The population ranking is not affected: Monaco is seventh there.

Frequently asked

What is the smallest country in the world?
Vatican City, in both rankings: under half a square kilometre and 882 inhabitants according to the game data. It is the only country that leads both by area and by population.
Which is the least populous country after the Vatican?
Tuvalu, with 9,646 inhabitants, ahead of Nauru (11,947) and Palau (17,695). By area the order differs: Nauru (20 km²) comes before Tuvalu (30 km²).
How many countries have fewer than one million inhabitants?
38 of the game’s {{countries}} countries, including eleven in Oceania and nine in Europe. The largest of them are Guyana, Suriname and Iceland.
Is Singapore a small country?
By area, yes: 728 km², the twentieth-smallest territory in the game. By population, no: a little over six million people, the 84th least populous country, and the highest density in the game.
