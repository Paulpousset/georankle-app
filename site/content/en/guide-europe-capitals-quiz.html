European capitals quiz: learn all 45 capitals in six blocks

Nobody memorises forty-five names in one go. You memorise six small groups and two or three well-identified mix-ups. Here is the split, built from the game data.

Guide · study method · game data, September 2026

The alphabetical list of European capitals, with its famous traps, already lives on this site: the capitals of Europe reference page. This page answers a different question: how to actually learn them when you are preparing a quiz, an exam, or are simply tired of missing Ljubljana.

The scope is the game's: {{count:europe}} countries, including Cyprus, Russia, the United Kingdom and Vatican City, with Turkey, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Kazakhstan counted in Asia. If your teacher or quiz draws the line elsewhere, a few capitals get added or removed, but the method does not change.

Six blocks instead of one list

The sub-region field in the game data splits the {{count:europe}} countries into six blocks of four to ten countries. It is a good working split: each block fits in working memory, and the countries of a block are neighbours on the map, so you can lean on geography rather than on recitation. Inside each row, capitals are sorted from the most to the least populous country, so you start with the ones you probably already know.

A block takes one ten-minute session. The biggest one, Northern Europe (ten countries), splits naturally in two: Scandinavia and Iceland on one side, Ireland, the United Kingdom and the three Baltic states on the other.

The capitals that share their country's name

Five European countries in the game give their capital their own name, in whole or in part: Luxembourg, Monaco, San Marino, Vatican City and Andorra (Andorra la Vella). They are city-states or microstates: the territory is so small that capital and country merge. That is five free answers, provided you know the question is not a trap. They take a minute to learn, yet they are the ones hasty players overthink.

The pairs everyone swaps

Mistakes in a capitals quiz are not random. They almost always come from pairs of countries with similar names.

  • Slovakia and Slovenia: Bratislava and Ljubljana get swapped constantly. A memory hook: Slovakia lies further east, and Bratislava is the only national capital that touches two other sovereign states, Austria and Hungary. Slovenia, for its part, opens onto the Adriatic.
  • Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia: from north to south, Tallinn, Riga, Vilnius. Three countries, three capitals, one geographical order to remember.
  • Serbia and Bosnia and Herzegovina: Belgrade and Sarajevo. Montenegro (Podgorica) and North Macedonia (Skopje) complete the block.
  • Romania and Bulgaria: Bucharest and Sofia. Bucharest starts like “Budapest” (Hungary): that is the other classic mix-up.

Spellings that trip you up

Even when you know the city, the spelling can cost you the answer. Ukraine's capital is spelled Kiev in French in the game and Kyiv in English, the official transliteration from Ukrainian. Moldova's is Chisinau in French and Chișinău in English. Andorra's capital is Andorra la Vella, Malta's is Valletta, Cyprus's is Nicosia — a city divided in two since 1974.

The famous exceptions, on their own

Three capitals are not the biggest city of their country and deserve separate treatment: Bern (Zurich is more populous), Amsterdam (the government sits in The Hague) and Brussels, which is both the capital of Belgium and the seat of the European institutions. Add Berlin, the unified capital since 1990 after forty years of Bonn and East Berlin.

A three-session plan

  1. Session 1: the easy blocks. Western and Central Europe: fourteen capitals, most of them already familiar. Goal: a clean run in Capitals mode, Europe zone.
  2. Session 2: the southern blocks. Southern and Southeast Europe: seventeen capitals, including the five “free” ones. Do the south first, then the Balkans.
  3. Session 3: North and East. Fourteen capitals, including the three Baltic states and Ukraine. Finish with a full tour of all {{count:europe}} countries.

Space the sessions two or three days apart and replay the first one the day after the third: that is spaced repetition, detailed in the study guide. The golden rule is the same as anywhere: a country you miss is a country you replay in the same session.

Frequently asked questions

How many European capitals are there in the game?
{{count:europe}}, one per country. The game counts Cyprus, Russia, the United Kingdom and Vatican City in Europe, and puts Turkey, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Kazakhstan in Asia.
What is the fastest way to learn the capitals of Europe?
Group them into six geographical blocks, start with the countries you know, then handle the look-alike name pairs (Slovakia/Slovenia, Romania/Hungary) separately, in short, spaced sessions.
Which European countries have a capital named after them?
Luxembourg, Monaco, San Marino, Vatican City and Andorra (Andorra la Vella): five countries whose capital repeats their name, all very small.
Why do people mix up Bratislava and Ljubljana?
Because Slovakia and Slovenia have nearly identical names and similar sizes. Bratislava, on the Danube, touches Austria and Hungary; Ljubljana lies further south-west, toward the Adriatic.
