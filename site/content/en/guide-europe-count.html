How many countries in Europe? From 42 to 51, depending on the definition

There is no single figure but five, and each rests on a choice: where the continent stops, and what counts as a country.

Guide · game data, October 2026

The question looks simple and is not. The guide How many countries are there in the world? explains why the answer moves from 193 to 195 depending on whether two observer states are counted. For Europe the disagreement is wider, because it concerns both the status of states and the edge of the continent, which is not a natural boundary. This page gives the count for each definition, computed from the game’s data, and says which one to use for a quiz.

Five counts, from narrowest to widest

{{table:europe-count:scenarios}}

The game files 45 countries under Europe: that is the count used by the modes and by the pages of this site, for instance in the Europe capitals quiz or the list of all European countries. Only one of them is not a UN member: the Holy See, that is the Vatican, which has observer status. Without it the figure drops to 44, the number most often given when people say “the countries of Europe”.

The countries on the edge

The gaps come from seven countries whose place in Europe is debated. Two are in the game’s Europe, five are filed under Asia:

{{table:capital-set:europe-borderline}}
  • Russia is counted in Europe because most of its people and its capital are there, even though most of its territory is in Asia. With 14 neighbours in the game it ties with China for the record.
  • Cyprus lies geographically closer to Asia but belongs to political Europe: the game files it under Europe.
  • Turkey straddles two continents, with a small part in Europe around Istanbul. The game files it under Western Asia.
  • Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan, in the South Caucasus, are filed under Western Asia by the game, although many atlases and European institutions treat them as European countries.
  • Kazakhstan has a small part of its territory west of the Ural River, technically in Europe. It sits in Central Asia in the game.

Adding those five countries to the game’s Europe gives 50. One country is still missing from the widest list: Kosovo, which declared independence in 2008, is recognised by many states but is not a UN member, and is not in the game. With it you reach 51, the ceiling of common lists.

The 45 countries by block

{{table:europe-count:blocks}}

The blocks in the table are the game’s own. Northern Europe gathers the Scandinavian countries, the Baltic states, Ireland, Iceland and the United Kingdom, which is why it is the biggest block and the one to split into smaller series when revising (see the Nordic flags).

Which figure to remember?

  • For a GeoG quiz: 45, because that is the list the game asks about.
  • For a conversation: 44, the UN members of that list, or “about forty” if you want to avoid ruling on Russia and Cyprus.
  • For a contest question: always ask for the definition. A statement that says “50 countries” almost certainly counts the Caucasus and Turkey.

Microstates weigh on the count: six countries on the list are under 1,000 km² (Andorra, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino, Malta and the Vatican), and each counts as much as a large country. That is the subject of the guide to microstates.

Frequently asked questions

How many countries are there in Europe?
It depends on the definition: 45 in the game, 44 if you keep only UN member states, up to 51 with the Caucasus, Turkey, Kazakhstan and Kosovo.
Is Russia a European country?
The game files it under Europe: its capital and most of its population are there, even though most of its territory lies in Asia.
Is Turkey in Europe or Asia?
Both, depending on the criterion. A small part of its territory is in Europe, but the game files it under Western Asia.
Why is Kosovo not in the game?
It is not a UN member, and the game follows a list of 195 countries made of the member states and two observers. It adds one country to the widest lists of Europe.
