Higher or Lower: which of these two countries is above?

Two countries, one criterion, one question: which is ahead? The winner stays on, a new country steps in, and the chain runs until your first mistake.

How it works

Two countries appear with a criterion — population, area, tourist arrivals, GDP — and you pick the one with the higher value.

The winning country stays on screen, a new challenger arrives, and the chain continues. A single mistake ends the run: the whole point is to go as far as you can.

The difficulty rises by itself, because the game gradually narrows the gaps. Comparing China and Luxembourg is trivial; comparing Kenya and Sudan on population is much less so. And the criterion changes on every question: a country that just won on area can lose the next round on tourism.

It is the mode that installs orders of magnitude best. After a few runs you instinctively know that Nigeria outweighs Germany on population, that Algeria is larger than Greenland, or that France receives more tourists than the United States.

Frequently asked

Does the criterion change every question?
Yes. A country that just won on population can lose the next round on area or tourism. That is what stops you playing on autopilot.
How many mistakes can you make?
One: the run ends on the first error. The score is the length of the chain.
Which criteria are used?
The same {{themes}} as Rankle: population, area, GDP, tourism, coastline length, UNESCO sites, and about thirty other public indicators.
