Uganda

No sea coast, yet it holds a good part of the largest lake in Africa.

Country profile · game data, October 2026

Uganda has 50.0 million people according to the game, thirty-first in the world, on 241,550 km², seventy-ninth by area. That works out at about 207 people per square kilometre, a high density for East Africa, where most people live in the fertile south around Lake Victoria.

The capital is Kampala, also the largest city. The trap is regional: Kampala, Nairobi and Dodoma are three neighbouring capitals whose names are easily swapped in a quiz. To keep them apart, start from Lake Victoria: Kampala is on its northern shore, Nairobi further east inland, Dodoma to the south.

The game lists five land neighbours: South Sudan to the north, Kenya to the east, Tanzania and Rwanda to the south, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west. The country is landlocked, with no direct access to the sea, but it shares Lake Victoria with Kenya and Tanzania, and the White Nile starts there near Jinja. The equator crosses the south of the country.

The flag has six horizontal bands of black, yellow and red, with a white disc in the centre bearing a crowned crane, the country’s emblematic bird. It is one of the easiest African flags to recognise, because few others carry an animal.
