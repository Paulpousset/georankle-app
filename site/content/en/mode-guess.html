How it works

A mystery country is drawn. You name a country, and the game compares. No vague hot-or-cold: each guess returns precise clues.

  • The continent — right or wrong, the clue that eliminates fastest;
  • The direction to travel from your guess, and the distance as the crow flies;
  • Population and area, larger or smaller than the target;
  • GDP per capita and life expectancy, which often separate two neighbours;
  • Whether it has a coastline, and its number of land borders.

Nine clues in all, recomputed on every attempt. None of them gives the answer alone; it is crossing them that does.

The right strategy is not to guess wildly but to grid-search: start with one country per continent to find the right one, then use direction and distance to close in, and finally population to separate the last candidates. It is exactly the reasoning you apply without thinking when reading a map — the mode makes it explicit.

The fewer guesses you use, the more you score. A player who knows their subregions finds most countries in four or five.

Frequently asked

What is the best strategy?
Start with one country per continent to identify the right one, then use direction and distance to close in, and population to separate the last candidates.
How many guesses does it usually take?
A player who knows the subregions usually gets there in four or five. The score depends directly on the number of attempts.
Are the clues always the same?
Yes: continent, direction, distance, population, area, GDP per capita, life expectancy, coastline and number of land borders. What changes is the hidden country — and in the daily challenge it is the same one for every player in the world.
