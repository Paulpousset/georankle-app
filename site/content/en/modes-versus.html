Today’s geography challenge, duels and the ladder

One run a day, the same for everyone, playable in two minutes with no account. And when two minutes are no longer enough, live duels and a seasonal ladder.

Geography works very well solo, but it becomes something else once you can compare. GeoG offers three ways to measure yourself against others, from the lightest to the most committed.

The daily challenge is the way in: the same run for every player on the planet, renewed every night, with a share grid to compare results without spoiling the answers. Online duels pit two players, or up to eight, against exactly the same questions, live or among friends. Ranked mode turns those duels into an ELO ladder with tiers and seasons, for those who want to know where they really stand.

Nothing forces you to move from one to the next. Many players only do the daily challenge, two minutes every morning, and that is exactly who it is for. The sections below explain how each format works.

The geography challenge of the day

One run a day, the same for everyone, playable in two minutes and with no account. Tomorrow it starts again with new countries.

Every day at midnight UTC, a new run is drawn. It is identical for every player on the planet: same countries, same questions, same order. That is what makes the result comparable — and worth arguing about with friends the same evening.

The run mixes the game's modes: a few flags, a few capitals, one location on the 3D globe, one silhouette. The draw is deterministic, derived from the date: neither the server nor the player can influence it, and replaying the same day gives exactly the same run.

At the end the game produces a share grid — a string of emoji summarising your answers without revealing the correct ones, the Wordle way. You can paste it anywhere: it tells your friends how you did without spoiling their game.

A streak builds day after day: it counts consecutive days played, and resets the moment you skip one. It is the mode that asks the least — two minutes — and the one people come back to most.

No account is needed to play. A free account only keeps your streak across devices.

Multiplayer geography game: duels and online matches

The same questions, at the same time, each on their own screen. One against one, or up to eight — and if nobody is around, there is still the phone you pass along.

Every mode in the game plays multiplayer. What changes is the format.

The duel

One against one, in real time. Both players get exactly the same questions, each on their own side, and the higher score takes the round. You invite a friend by link, or let the game find an opponent. If nobody turns up, a bot steps in so the game starts anyway.

Matches for up to eight

The same idea, extended: up to eight players in one room, on any mode. Everyone answers on their own side, and the standings update round after round.

The custom game

Instead of a single mode, you compose your own sequence: three rounds of flags, two of silhouettes, one of borders. The game runs the programme you chose. It is the format closest to a pub quiz.

The league

For groups that play regularly, a private league offers three challenges a day, identical for all its members, with daily, monthly and all-time standings. You create one in seconds and invite by link.

Local mode

No connection and no account: one phone, passed between players. Everyone plays their turn, the best total wins. It is the format for a car journey or a table.

Ranked mode: climbing the tiers

An ELO ladder, six tiers, series that lengthen as you climb, and a season that puts it all back in play. It is the most demanding format in the game.

Ranked mode pits two players against each other over a multi-mode series of rounds: flags, capitals, globe, silhouettes, borders, Rankle. The winner of the series gains rating points, the loser gives some up.

An ELO, not a win counter

The calculation is a classic ELO, the same principle as in chess: beating a much higher-rated player is worth a lot, beating a much lower-rated one almost nothing. So the number of games you play does not move your tier — only the quality of the opponents you beat.

Longer series at the top

Series length depends on tier: five rounds up to Silver, seven up to Diamond, nine above. The higher you go, the less luck weighs — a long series leaves little room for a fluke on one flag.

Seasons

The ladder runs in seasons. At the close, the best players are rewarded and a new season starts. That lets a player who arrived mid-way aim for the top without having to make up a year's head start.

The tiers and their thresholds

Six tiers, from Bronze to Master. The thresholds are the game’s own, read directly from its code — they move if the balancing moves.

Frequently asked

When does the daily challenge change?
At midnight UTC. Every player in the world then receives the same new run.
Do I need an account?
No. The daily challenge starts straight in the browser, with no sign-up. A free account only keeps your streak across devices.
Can I replay yesterday’s challenge?
No: each day has its own run, playable only that day. That is what gives the consecutive-day streak its meaning.
How does the share grid work?
It turns your results into emoji, without revealing the answers. Paste it into a message or a social post: friends see how you did but keep the surprise.
Do I need an account to play online?
Yes for duels, matches and leagues — you have to be identifiable between rounds. Local mode and the daily challenge play without one.
What if nobody joins?
A bot steps in after a short delay so the game starts anyway. Its level adapts to yours.
Can several people play on one phone?
Yes, that is local mode: each player takes a turn on the same device, with no connection and no account.
What is a league?
A private group with three challenges a day, identical for all its members, and daily, monthly and all-time standings. You invite by link.
How does the ladder work?
By ELO, as in chess: beating a higher-rated player earns a lot of points, beating a much lower-rated one almost none. The number of games played has no direct effect on your tier.
How many tiers are there?
Six: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond and Master. The table above gives the exact thresholds.
Why are the series longer at the top?
To reduce luck. Five rounds up to Silver, seven up to Diamond, nine above: the longer the series, the more the better player wins.
What happens at the end of a season?
The best players are rewarded and a new season starts, which lets a newcomer aim for the top of the ladder.
