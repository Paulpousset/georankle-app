The hardest countries to place on a map

They are not the smallest, nor the most remote. They are the ones that never appear in the news, never on an itinerary, and never in a textbook.

Guide · updated August 2026

Ask someone to place France on a map: they will manage. Ask them to place Kiribati, and they will probably point at the ocean and hope. Between the two there is no difference in geographic difficulty — Kiribati is no more hidden than France — but a difference in exposure.

What makes a country “invisible”

A country enters our memory through four channels: its population, its economic weight, tourism, and its presence online. A country that fails on all four disappears, whatever its area. That is why Kazakhstan, the ninth-largest country on Earth, is better located than Papua New Guinea, and why Monaco — two square kilometres — is better known than Chad.

How to learn them

The worst method is to revise them one by one in alphabetical order: they have no anchor, so there is nothing to hold on to. The right method is to take them in geographic blocks, leaning on what you already know.

  • Micronesia — Palau, the Federated States of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands, Nauru, Kiribati: north of the equator, east of the Philippines. Five countries, one zone.
  • Polynesia — Samoa, Tonga, Tuvalu: further east and south, between Fiji and Tahiti.
  • Melanesia — Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, Fiji: north-east of Australia, in an arc.
  • The Lesser Antilles — Antigua, Dominica, Saint Lucia, Grenada, Saint Vincent, Saint Kitts: a chain running north to south, between Puerto Rico and Venezuela.
  • The Gulf of Guinea — São Tomé and Príncipe, Equatorial Guinea: two states in the elbow of Africa.

Five blocks, about twenty countries, and most of the problem is solved. The 3D globe is the most effective mode for this exercise: unlike a flat map, it shows real distances, and you finally grasp that Kiribati spreads across more than three thousand kilometres.

Frequently asked

What is the least known country in the world?
By the game's familiarity indicator, Tuvalu — 9,600 inhabitants, 30 km², in the middle of the Pacific. It closes a ranking dominated by Oceania.
Why is Oceania so poorly known?
Because its countries combine all four invisibility factors: tiny populations, small economies, minimal tourism and low online presence. Their area makes no difference.
How do you memorise the Pacific countries?
In blocks rather than one by one: Micronesia to the north-west, Polynesia to the east, Melanesia north-east of Australia. Three zones, about a dozen countries.
