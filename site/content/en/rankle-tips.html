Five rules that hold

1. Read all eight criteria before placing the first country

The criteria are shown from the start; the countries arrive one by one. Before touching anything, spot the rare slots — those where only a handful of countries are good: highest point, UNESCO sites, tourist arrivals, Olympic medals — and the wide slots, where half the planet does fine: access to electricity, urban population, life expectancy.

2. Keep the rare slots for those who have nothing else

A country that is good on one criterion only must get it. A country good everywhere can settle for an average slot. When a big country arrives early, resist giving it the best slot: ask who else, among the {{countries}}, might need it.

3. Be wary of microstates

Monaco, Liechtenstein, Qatar, Singapore: often first on a wealth or density criterion, and with no data — hence 200 — on sport, forest or army criteria. A misplaced microstate costs more than any other country.

4. Accept sacrificing a country

With eight countries and eight slots, someone ends up in a bad slot. Choose who: the country that is average everywhere takes a rank of 60 without drama, where an extreme country would take 190.

And in Streak?

The Streak variant flips the logic: a single country, four criteria, and you must find the true best — not the best given the others. There the reflex is the right answer; raw knowledge of the extremes is what makes a long run.

Frequently asked

Do I need to know the exact figures?
No. Rankle compares ranks, not values. Knowing that Brazil is in the top five by area is enough; knowing it covers 8.5 million km² adds nothing.
How does the game find the optimal placement?
By examining the 40,320 possible arrangements of eight countries over eight criteria (with pruning that abandons hopeless branches early) and keeping the one with the lowest sum of ranks. It is the same computation that produces the grid on this page.
Why is my efficiency low when every country is on a good criterion?
Because efficiency measures the whole grid. Eight “good” choices that steal the best slots from each other are worth less than an arrangement where two countries are sacrificed so the other six are perfect.
